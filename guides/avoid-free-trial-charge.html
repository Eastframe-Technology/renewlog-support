How to avoid being charged after a free trial

Most trials that ask for a payment method renew by themselves. The safest habit is to decide on day one: note the end date, set a reminder a few days ahead, and cancel at the place you signed up.

On the day you sign up

  1. Read the end date on the screen or in the receipt. Screenshot it. It is the one date you need.
  2. Note the price that follows, and how often. "Then $6.99 per week" is a different decision from "then $6.99 per month".
  3. Note where you signed up. Google Play, the App Store or the service's own website. That is where you will have to cancel.
  4. Set a reminder. Three days before the end works well. It leaves time if you are busy on the day.

Working out the date

If you only know the length, count from the sign-up day as day zero. A 7-day trial started on 1 November usually ends on 8 November. A one-month trial started on 31 January ends on the last day of February.

The free trial calculator does this for you. It also gives you the last safe day to cancel and a calendar event for the reminder. It works on calendar dates, so a daylight saving change cannot move the answer.

Treat the day before the end date as your deadline. The charge can come at any time on the last day, and the service's clock may not match yours.

Where to cancel

Cancel where you started the trial. That place holds the billing, and nothing else can stop it.

  • Google Play: in the Play Store app, tap your profile picture, then Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions. Pick the service and cancel.
  • App Store: on an iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions. Pick the service and cancel. Apple asks you to do this at least 24 hours before the trial ends.
  • The service's website: look in your account or billing settings. Some services started on the web can only be cancelled there.

There is more detail in how to find what you are subscribed to.

Mistakes that lead to a charge

  • Deleting the app. Deleting or uninstalling an app does not cancel its subscription. The billing carries on without the app.
  • Waiting until the last evening. You may already have been charged by then.
  • Assuming an email will warn you. Some services send one and some do not. Do not rely on it.
  • Cancelling on the wrong account. If you do not see the subscription, you may have signed up with another Google or Apple account.
  • Removing your card. That does not cancel a subscription, and the service may keep trying to bill you.

Can you cancel straight away?

Often, yes, and it is the safest way to avoid a forgotten charge. On many subscriptions you keep access until the trial ends, even after you cancel. Not on all of them, though. Some services end the trial as soon as you cancel, so read the confirmation screen before you tap the final button. If it says access stops immediately, keep your reminder instead and cancel later.

If you were already charged

  1. Cancel now, so that it does not happen again.
  2. Ask for a refund quickly. Google Play and the App Store both have a refund request process for purchases made through them. For a trial started on the web, write to the service. Refunds are not guaranteed, and asking soon gives you the best chance.
  3. Be clear and brief. Say when you signed up, that you meant to stop at the end of the trial, and that you have not used the paid period, if that is true.
  4. Talk to your bank last. A disputed charge can be an option if a service refuses and you believe the charge was wrong, but your bank can tell you what applies to you.

If you try a lot of things

One reminder is easy. Five at once is where people slip. Keep them all in one place with their end dates, whether that is a calendar, a note or an app.
